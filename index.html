Animals fluidly switch between gaits — trotting on rough ground, bounding over obstacles — picking the right skill for the terrain ahead. Robots, so far, have not.


APT-RL (Action Pretrained Transformer-based Reinforcement Learning) closes this gap. One onboard policy lets a quadruped robot see the terrain coming, choose the right gait and motor skill on its own, and transition between them smoothly — at high speed, with no external sensors or computation.


The result: KAIST HOUND jumps a 60 cm box at 4.25 m/s, leaps down a three-step staircase at 6 m/s, and traverses stairs, hurdles, stepping stones, gaps, and fallen trees — all with a single policy.

How APT-RL Works

The core idea: instead of learning everything from scratch with reinforcement learning, first pretrain a library of locomotion skills from cheap, physically valid motion data — then let RL learn to use those skills on hard terrain.

Trajectory optimization with a simplified 2D robot model generates 180,000 trajectories (15.5 hours of motion) in just 8 minutes — each one annotated with the joint torques needed to execute it. No animal motion capture, no expensive simulation rollouts.

Training then proceeds in three stages:

  1. Learn the skills. A Transformer-based VAE compresses the trotting and bounding trajectories into a shared latent space, and torque decoders learn to turn any point in that space into motor commands — purely by supervised learning.
  2. Learn to use them. An RL policy picks latent actions (which skill to execute) and adds a small auxiliary correction on top — letting the robot adapt pretrained flat-ground skills to stairs, gaps, and obstacles it has never seen.
  3. Learn to see. A student network learns to reproduce the terrain understanding of a privileged teacher, using only real sensors — a depth camera and 2D LiDAR — so the whole system runs onboard, zero-shot, in the real world.

The robot decides for itself when to trot and when to bound — reading the terrain ahead, its own body state, and the commanded speed. Low obstacle? Trot through it. High step at speed? Switch to bounding and jump. No human picks the gait.

Tested in the Wild

Urban — 1.1 km across a university campus. Multi-level stairs, grass patches, and ramps. The robot trotted down stairs at 1 m/s, bounded down them at 4.3 m/s, and hit a peak of 6 m/s leaping down a three-step staircase.

Wild — 0.34 km of forest trail. Fallen trees, exposed roots, uneven logs, and slippery leaf-covered ground. The robot bounded over elevated logs, switched to trotting on irregular ground, and sped back up when commanded — all decided onboard, in real time.

Beyond the Training Data

The pretrained skills only cover flat-ground trotting and bounding in 2D. Everything else — jumping, climbing, recovering — emerges from how the policy combines the pretrained skills with its learned auxiliary corrections.

Jumping a log it has never seen. On flat ground, the pretrained decoder torque does most of the work. The moment the jump begins, the auxiliary torque takes over — producing a motion that exists nowhere in the pretraining data.

Surviving a broken leg. When a leg fractures mid-walk — an event never encountered in training — the policy cuts the decoder torque on the affected joint and compensates with auxiliary torque, reshaping the leg trajectory to keep the robot balanced.

KAIST HOUND

We deploy APT-RL on KAIST HOUND, an in-house quadruped robot designed for fast and efficient locomotion. The robot is equipped with a D435 depth camera and a 2D LiDAR sensor for onboard perception, running all computation onboard.

To handle high-impact locomotion at speeds exceeding 4 m/s, we designed custom 3D-printed mechanical vibration absorbers mounted between the robot head and the LiDAR sensor, reducing impact loads that would otherwise exceed 10 g and cause sensor malfunctions.
